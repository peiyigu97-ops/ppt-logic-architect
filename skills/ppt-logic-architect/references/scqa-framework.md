# SCQA Framework

A narrative opening and progression: **Situation, Complication, Question, Answer**. Associated with the introduction pattern of Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle. It earns the audience's attention by connecting what they already accept to a tension they must resolve.

## 1. The four parts

| Part | What it is | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** (情境) | The stable context the audience already accepts | Would the audience nod? It is uncontroversial and brief |
| **Complication** (冲突) | A change or tension that makes the situation unsatisfactory | Is it real, specific, and caused by something that actually happened? |
| **Question** (疑问) | The question the complication raises | Is it *this audience's* question, and does the deck answer it? |
| **Answer** (回答) | The thesis | Does it answer exactly the question asked? |

## 2. Two scales

**Deck level**: SCQA frames the opening (one to three slides). The Answer is the central thesis; the rest of the deck supports it.

**Section level (relay)**: each section has a mini SCQA, and the Answer of one section creates the Complication of the next. This is a way to make progression explicit.

```
Section 1 Answer: "The sensor saves energy in the lab."
   -> Section 2 Complication: "But the lab used 70% rest traces, not daily life."
   -> Section 2 Question: "Does the saving hold with realistic activity mixes?"
```

Use the relay when you need to name a cause-effect or contrast between sections. If you cannot write the next Complication from the previous Answer, the order is arbitrary.

## 3. Variants

- **Answer first** (Answer, then S, C as justification): for executives who want the conclusion immediately.
- **Question first**: for academic or defense settings where the research question is the entry point.
- **Skip the Situation**: when the audience knows the context. Open on the Complication.

## 4. When to use it, and when not to

Use when the audience needs shared context and a reason to care, and a real complication exists.

Do not use when:
- The audience already knows the context and wants results.
- There is no tension, only a sequence of tasks.
- It would only be a ritual opener that adds slides and no logic.
- The whole deck is a research report whose structure is Question, Method, Results, Interpretation. SCQA may open it but should not shape it.

## 5. Pitfalls

- **Fake complication**: "Companies face many challenges." It must be specific to this problem.
- **Overlong Situation**: five slides of background. Cut to what the Complication needs.
- **Wrong question**: the Question reflects the presenter's interest, not the audience's.
- **Answer mismatch**: the Complication is about cost and the Answer is about usability.
- **Every deck is SCQA**: a sign of template habit.

## 6. Output

Write it as four short lines in the Master Outline and map each to slides:

```
S: ...
C: ...
Q: ...
A: <the thesis>
```
