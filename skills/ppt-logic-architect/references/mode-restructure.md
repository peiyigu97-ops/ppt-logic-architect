# Mode B - RESTRUCTURE

Reorganize an existing deck or outline so its logic works, while keeping as much valid content as possible.

The governing rule: **the author's content is an asset, not scrap.** You change the order, framing, and gaps. You do not discard material without a reason the user can read and overrule.

## Procedure

### 1. Inventory the existing deck (Phase 1)
Build a slide table before judging anything:

| # | Title as written | Slide type | What it claims or shows | Evidence present? | Apparent role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Slide type: title, agenda, divider, claim, evidence, process, summary, appendix, other.
- "Evidence present?": Yes / Partial / No, and what kind.
- "Apparent role": your best reading of why the slide is there. If you cannot tell, write "unclear" - do not guess a role to make the slide look justified.
- Include speaker notes if available; they often hold the argument that the slides lack.

### 2. Identify the original structure (Phase 1-2)
- The sections as presented (agenda, dividers) versus the sections that are really there (clusters of related slides). Note where they differ.
- The ordering principle actually in use: chronological, by topic, by source, by the author's workflow, or none.
- How information is organized: by topic labels, by claims, by process stages.

### 3. Reconstruct the as-is argument (Phase 2)
State what the deck argues *as it stands*, charitably but honestly: the thesis it appears to hold, the supporting points it makes, the evidence it gives. If you cannot find a thesis, say so. That is itself the main finding. Show this to the user before proposing anything new.

### 4. Diagnose (Phase 1-2, detail in Phase 5 passes)
List, with slide numbers:
- **Argument breaks**: a step from one claim to the next with nothing connecting them.
- **Duplicates**: same claim or same evidence shown more than once without a new purpose.
- **Purposeless slides**: no identifiable claim, or nothing depends on them.
- **Conclusion-evidence mismatches**: a conclusion that the evidence shown does not support, or goes beyond.
- **Orphans**: evidence with no claim, or claims with no evidence.
- **Misplaced slides**: content that belongs in another section.
- **Missing pieces**: a question the audience will ask that the deck never answers.

### 5. Propose the new structure (Phase 2-3)
New thesis (keep the author's if it is sound; sharpen it if it is vague; narrow it if the evidence is weaker), new section list with roles, and the progression between sections. Explain each major move in one line: what was wrong, what the change fixes.

### 6. Map old to new (Phase 3-4)
**Every original slide appears in the mapping table.** No exceptions.

| Old # | Old title | Disposition | New # | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Dispositions:

| Disposition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| KEEP | Same slide, possibly moved |
| REHEAD | Content kept, headline rewritten to an assertion |
| MERGE | Combined with one or more slides; list them |
| SPLIT | One slide becomes several; each part has its own purpose |
| MOVE | Same content, different position or section |
| APPENDIX | Valid but not needed in the main line |
| REMOVE | Only with a written reason (duplicate of slide X, no claim and no dependent slide, out of scope for the stated purpose). Needs user approval |

When unsure, use APPENDIX, not REMOVE.

### 7. Write the new slide cards (Phase 4)
Full ten-field cards for the new structure. For each card, say which old slide(s) it comes from. For slides that need evidence the original does not have, write the card with the requirement and a `G#`, not with invented evidence.

### 8. Audit and deliver (Phase 5-6)
Audit the new structure with `references/logic-audit.md`. Deliver the five files; `slide-by-slide.md` includes the mapping table.

## RESTRUCTURE rules

- **Preserve valid content.** Keep the author's terminology, examples and data wherever they hold up.
- **No unexplained deletions.** Every REMOVE has a reason and a user approval; the default is APPENDIX.
- **Do not fix gaps by inventing.** If the new structure needs evidence the author never had, record the gap.
- **Do not silently change meaning.** If REHEAD sharpens a headline, the new claim must not exceed what the slide's evidence shows. When it would, narrow the claim, or flag a gap.
- **Show the as-is argument before the new one**, so the user can confirm you understood their intent.
- **A deck with a sound structure and weak spots is an AUDIT case.** Say so and offer AUDIT rather than restructuring for its own sake.
